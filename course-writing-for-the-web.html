Module 1 · Writing a Sentence Someone Finishes

25 min

The first line decides whether anyone reads the second.

  • Cutting throat-clearing openings
  • Writing hooks that promise something specific
  • Project: rewrite five bad opening lines

Module 2 · Structure Before Style

35 min

A clear structure forgives average sentences; no structure ruins great ones.

  • Outlining without over-planning
  • Ordering ideas so nothing needs re-explaining later
  • One idea per paragraph, genuinely

Module 3 · Cutting Without Losing Meaning

40 min

The core editing skill this whole course is built around.

  • Spotting sentences that repeat themselves
  • Killing adverbs that are covering for a weak verb
  • Project: cut a 500-word draft to 350

Module 4 · Writing for How People Actually Read Online

38 min

Nobody reads a webpage top to bottom like a novel.

  • Scannable structure: headers, short paragraphs, lists
  • Writing subheads that work if that's all someone reads
  • SEO basics that don't wreck your writing

Module 5 · Editing Someone Else's Draft (and Your Own)

32 min

Editing your own work is the hardest edit you'll do.

  • Reading your draft like a stranger would
  • Giving feedback that's specific, not just 'make it better'
  • Project: edit a peer's draft with real notes

Module 6 · Voice Without Gimmicks

30 min

A distinct voice, without leaning on quirks that get old fast.

  • Finding your natural sentence rhythm
  • Avoiding forced jokes and filler asides
  • Consistency across a full piece

Module 7 · Publishing and Getting Read

28 min

Writing that never gets read didn't finish the job.

  • Where and how to publish for the audience you want
  • Titles that don't oversell the piece
  • Sharing your work without over-apologizing for it

Module 8 · Final Piece Workshop

35 min

One last pass before it goes out into the world.

  • Line-editing your final draft
  • A final structure check against your outline
  • Final review session with your mentor
What you'll walk away with

Four published pieces, edited down to what matters

By the end of week five you'll have drafted, edited, and published four pieces — each one shorter and sharper than your first draft, with a habit of cutting that sticks after the course ends.

Final project: A published piece, start to finish

Due week 5

Take one idea from a rough note to a fully edited, published piece — 600 to 900 words. It needs a clear hook, no filler paragraphs, and a structure a reader can follow without rereading a sentence.

  • Submitted through the course dashboard for mentor review
  • One round of written feedback before final grading
  • Passing projects earn a certificate and get added to your public profile
